# The Studio — vision

## Purpose

**AI holds the structure. Humans hold judgment.**

Building software stopped being the bottleneck — AI can write the code.
The bottleneck now is everything around the code: remembering what was
decided and why, keeping four projects' worth of context alive, knowing
what to work on next, and not scattering. That's organizational work,
and it's exactly the work a scattered human brain is worst at and an AI
is quietly excellent at.

The studio is an operating system for the smallest possible company:
the minimum number of humans, with an AI chief-of-staff wearing the PM
and Engineer hats — not just writing code, but *organizing the work*:
owning the backlog, scoping the sprints, shielding the focus, keeping
the records. The humans keep vision, priorities, taste, and final say.

It is designed around a human's cognition, not around throughput.
Every ceremony is capped at what will actually survive a busy,
distracted founder — because a system that dies in a month optimizes
nothing.

## The thesis

1. **The scarce resource is attention, not code.** Optimize for the
   human's focus and the system's memory; code output takes care of
   itself.
2. **Structure must live outside the human's head** — in files, cards,
   and logs that any session (human or AI) can pick up cold. If it only
   exists in a chat, it doesn't exist.
3. **Small, shipped, tested beats big, planned, pending.** One paper
   cut at a time; done means live in production *and* verified by a
   human on a real device.
4. **The AI's PM job includes saying no** — to scope creep, to
   mid-sprint derailments, and to the founder. An inbox that catches
   every idea is what makes "no" cheap: nothing is lost, so nothing
   has to be chased now.
5. **Evidence over claims.** Shipped logs, sprint plans, and decision
   records are the portfolio; the system should always be able to show
   its work.

## Scope

- One founder today; the design deliberately generalizes to a tiny
  team (the load-bearing idea is *minimum humans + AI holding the
  structure*, not the headcount of one).
- A portfolio of small products under one OS — currently four live
  apps — rather than a single bet.
- The studio itself is product zero: it improves through one retro
  question per sprint, not through its own planning ceremony.

## Explicit non-goals

- **Not an autonomous company.** No agent spends money, ships, or
  decides priorities without a human. Autonomy is not the point;
  leverage is.
- **Not a code generator or app builder.** Tools that write code are
  interchangeable parts; this is the governance layer above them.
- **Not a framework competing on automation breadth.** No installers,
  no 50 workflows. Depth of fit for one real operation over breadth
  of features for hypothetical ones.
- **Not productivity theater.** No standups, no status reports, no
  metrics kept for their own sake. If a ritual doesn't change a
  decision, it's cut.

## The one-line test

Months from now, the founder opens the dashboard and sees several live
products, a calm backlog, and a visible trail of shipped work — and
can't remember the last time a project "fell apart" from disorganization.
Anything that doesn't serve that picture is out.
